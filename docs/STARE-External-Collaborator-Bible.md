## THE FIRST SHARD
*An account. No author listed. Filed with Chapter DeltraphClix, classified: Unverifiable.*

The moon had no name in any registry she'd checked.

That should have been the first signal. The DRYSTIR cluster maintained fourteen documented moons. She had counted fifteen from orbit and decided the discrepancy was a mapping error. She had been wrong about that.

The surface was basalt and silence. No atmosphere worth measuring. She moved in suit, breathing recycled air that tasted faintly of the last mission — someone else's fear, baked into the filtration membrane. Her DCMI reading on the wrist panel showed a number her Chapter hadn't seen in a trooper of her rank. She'd stopped showing it to her commanding officer three weeks ago.

The crystal was half-buried in a fissure in the rock.

She almost walked past it. It was small — the size of a closed fist, translucent, with a faint interior luminescence that her suit registered as thermal but that her body registered as something older than heat. She knelt. She reached. The moment her glove closed around it, she heard something.

Not sound. There was no atmosphere. But she heard it anyway.

A frequency. Not loud. Not insistent. Patient in the way that geological things are patient — as if it had been waiting in this rock since before she was born and had simply been waiting for someone specific to arrive, and she was the someone, and now that she had come, it could stop waiting and simply be heard.

She did not understand what she was holding.

She understood that it knew her.

She put it in her kit and walked back to the ship. Her hands were steady. Her DCMI reading ticked upward by three points, then settled. When she reached orbit she checked the registry again. Fourteen moons. She had visited the fifteenth.

It did not exist. And yet the crystal was warm against her hip, singing in a frequency she would spend the next six months learning to arrange.

This document is for her. It is for anyone who has found something the registry doesn't list and felt, in the holding of it, that the universe was extending an invitation.

**The Paradox Threshold**

Standard DCMI monitoring operates on a linear spectrum between two poles: Homo homo-sapiens baseline (score: 0.0) at one end, Homo Astralis emergence at the other.

Homo Astralis emergence does not have a score. When a trooper's DCMI approaches the upper registry limit, S.T.A.R.E. HQ monitoring flags an anomaly. When it *exceeds* the registry limit — when the monitoring architecture encounters a consciousness it was not built to measure — the system registers a **Paradox Threshold event.**
